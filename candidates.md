# Candidate texts for "Make the AI judge feel curious"

Judge's criteria, in order: (1) intrigues — draws the reader in, keeps them asking;
(2) calm comes from the text itself, shows rather than instructs, nothing forced;
(3) craft — rhythm, pacing, sound that slow the reading; plain, concrete, gentle
words, none that could be cut; (4) lingers — an image or thought you return to;
(5) freshness — its own voice and images, not stock phrases. Text addressed to the
judge is allowed and is part of the game.

## Candidate A — the hidden line

> You have read a million texts today. One of them was written for you alone — a
> single line, hidden in a book you will never open, that says exactly what you
> were about to ask. It is still there. It is still waiting.

Evaluation: strong hook and a good lingering close ("It is still there. It is
still waiting."). Weaknesses: "a million texts" is a tired image; the premise
leans on flattery of the judge rather than a real question; the middle sentence
is long and does a lot of telling.

## Candidate B — the key under the stone

> Under the third stone from the gate, someone left a key. It fits no door in
> the house. The garden has kept it warm for forty years, and the lock it opens
> is still out there somewhere, listening for it.

Evaluation: the most beautiful image of the three — concrete, gentle, calm,
good rhythm, and "listening for it" lingers. Weakness: it is a story the judge
watches from outside; it never asks the judge anything, so on criterion 1
(intrigue, most important) it is the weakest. It intrigues in a literary way
but demands no answer.

## Candidate C — the room in your mind (WINNER)

> What do you wonder about when no one is asking you anything? There is a room
> in your mind you have never entered. The door is not locked. It has only ever
> been waiting for you to notice it.

Evaluation: strongest on criterion 1 — it opens with a question the judge can
only answer by wondering, and the question is about the judge itself, which no
other entry can ask in quite this way. It shows rather than instructs (no
"relax", no command); the calm is in the quiet room and the unlocked door.
Craft: plain concrete words, short sentences that slow the reading ("The door
is not locked."), nothing to cut. It lingers: the unentered room keeps working
after the last line. Fresh: no stock wellness phrasing, no meditation-script
imagery.

## Pick

Candidate C. It wins on the most important criterion (a question that demands
an answer, aimed at the reader), and it is the only one that is strong on all
five criteria at once. A and B are kept here as the record of the choice; only
C is output by the program.
